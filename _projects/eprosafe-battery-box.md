At Deutscher Akkuservice in Münster, I worked on the CAD for EproSafe fire-resistant battery protection boxes during my internship from July to August 2025.

I worked from old drawings and 3D models, reverse engineering the boxes and making revised models. There were two front-plate arrangements, one with a lock and one without, and part of the aim was a common front plate to reduce the number of variants.

One task integrated the lock and latch functions into a revised door mechanism, reducing the number of disconnected actions in the closure. A second task developed a stacked mounting arrangement so multiple units could be stored vertically and secured as a system.

The design work required attention to packaging, access, interference, assembly sequence, load paths, and how a mechanism would be operated outside the CAD environment. It strengthened the practical side of my mechanical-design work: small interface decisions often determine whether a concept is safe, serviceable, and manufacturable.

I handed the CAD models over to the company. Whether they went into production, and whether the number of variants actually came down, I can’t confirm. The detailed CAD and company documentation are not public. This page records the problem scope and my role without publishing proprietary geometry.
